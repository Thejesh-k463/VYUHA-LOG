The difference

Most journals are a spreadsheet with charts.

Vyuha is a local-first trading back-office. Eight things nothing else on the Indian market does properly.

🇮🇳

Charges to the rupee

STT, exchange txn, SEBI, stamp, IPFT, GST, DP, pledge — computed per broker × segment × exchange from an editable rate table and reconciled against real broker files. Money is stored as integer paise, so there is no float drift, ever.

💸

MTF done right

Interest accrues on the broker-funded portion only, with the correct T+1 day count. Tools that assume the full position value quietly overstate your cost. See real ROI on your own capital — and a warning when interest has eaten your entire paper gain.

🪜

Positions built in tranches

Scale in with a stop on each entry, scale out in parts, see per-leg R. Every other journal models a trade as one entry and one exit — which is not how anyone actually trades.

📋

Knows today's SEBI regime

A compliance radar reads your open book against the post-2024 F&O rules — the +2% expiry-day ELM on short options, the loss of calendar-spread benefit on expiry, which indices still have weeklies, and how close you sit to the ₹1,500 cr index limit now snapshotted intraday.

🔍

Analytics that don't flatter you

The Trade Craft cockpit plots your win-rate against your payoff ratio on the breakeven curve — so you see whether you win big and lose small, or the reverse. Stop-loss exits are classified against the stop you recorded. Every claim carries its sample size, and reports say "descriptive, not prescriptive."

🧠

A second brain that can't hallucinate

Vyuha Intelligence is a deterministic insight engine, not a chatbot: every insight is computed from your own record, refuses to exist below its sample floor, and carries its coverage with the claim ("SL recorded on 12 of 40 losers"). Fully offline. It never invents a rupee and never tells you what to buy — by design.

⚖️

Turnover, both ways

Your broker's tax report and current ICAI guidance compute F&O turnover differently — on a real book they disagreed by 6.5–8.7×, enough to straddle the audit threshold. Vyuha shows both figures, labelled, with an audit read on each: the exact table to put in front of your CA.

🔒

Nothing leaves your machine

No login. No cloud. No telemetry. No analytics SDKs. Everything lives in one SQLite file on your disk — copy it and you have backed up your entire trading life.

Questions

Straight answers.

Does my trade data ever leave my computer?

Not unless you switch on a feature that sends it — and each one says so before you can. There is no account, no server and no telemetry, and everything lives in a single SQLite file on your disk. The only request Vyuha makes on its own is download-only: at launch it asks GitHub whether a newer signed release exists and fetches the licence-revocation list — it sends no account, no identifier and no data, and this one cannot be switched off. Everything else is off by default and yours to enable: an end-of-day bhavcopy download for mark-to-market prices; broker pulls that talk only to your own broker's API; and a daily Telegram digest of your own recorded numbers — the one path that uploads anything, and it is enabled only behind a disclosure that says plainly the content goes to Telegram's servers.

What is free, and what am I paying for?

Your record is free. The intelligence about it is paid. Free forever: recording closed trades, all six broker importers, the dashboard and P&L calendar, staged positions, playbooks, chart screenshots, the trade calculator, backups and full CSV/JSON export. Payment unlocks the analytics layer — the portfolio risk cockpit, Arjun's Eye and the edge/discipline reports, the options-seller journal, the tax suite (Tax Summary, ITR pack, advance tax, harvesting, AIS reconcile), broker-cost and cross-broker MTF comparison, PDF reports, and live open-position tracking with stops and targets.

The part that matters: every trade you have already recorded stays readable, editable and exportable without a key, forever. If you never buy, you still own your journal — not a read-only screenshot of it.

Which brokers can I import from?

Six brokers are auto-detected: Zerodha (tradebook and Console P&L), Dhan, Groww, Angel One, Upstox and Paytm Money — tradebook or aggregated P&L exports. Any other broker's CSV goes through the column mapper, where you say which column is which. And Zerodha, Dhan, Angel One and Upstox can also be pulled directly over their APIs (Import → Connect broker) — one click after market close. Brokers with no API of their own — Groww, Paytm Money, Kotak — can pull same-day through OpenAlgo, an open-source bridge you run on your own computer: off by default, and your broker credentials go into your own OpenAlgo instance, never into Vyuha. Everything is de-duplicated, and charges are recomputed from your own rate card so you can see exactly where your broker's number differs.

Can I move it to a new computer?

Yes. Copy your database file across and re-paste your key. If your key was locked to a specific machine at your request, message us with the new Machine ID shown in Settings → License and we will re-issue it.

Beyond that, purchases are final, because the trial already gave you the whole product for nothing. If we refund you, your journal still works: every trade you recorded stays readable, editable and exportable.
